German Cases Overview

* Four cases: Nominativ, Genitiv, Dativ, Akkusativ
* Subject is always Nominativ; objects take Genitiv, Dativ, or Akkusativ
* Only one Nominativ per sentence (the subject)
* Multiple objects possible, but only one Dativ after a verb (unless repeated with commas)

German Sentence Structure: The Golden Position

* Verb always sits in the second position, no exceptions
* Subject and object can be swapped freely around the verb
   * "Der Hund fängt den Ball" = "Den Ball fängt der Hund" (both correct)
   * In English this would change meaning; in German the case articles clarify who does what
* Three required parts: subject, verb, object

Articles: Bestimmt, Unbestimmt, Negativ, Possessiv

* Definite (bestimmter Artikel): der (m), die (f), das (n), die (pl)
* Indefinite (unbestimmter Artikel): ein (m), eine (f), ein (n), no plural form
   * No plural for "ein" because "ein" means one
* Negation (negativer Artikel): kein is just a K in front of ein
   * kein (m/n), keine (f/pl)
   * e.g. kein Mann, keine Frau, kein Haus
* Possessive follows the same endings as the indefinite article (mein, dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer, Ihr)

Dativ vs. Akkusativ

* The verb decides the case, not word order
* 37 key dative verbs to memorize (geben, schenken, erklären, kaufen, bringen, schicken, leihen, etc.)
   * If a verb is not on the dative list, it takes Akkusativ
   * 80% of all German cases are Akkusativ; default to Akkusativ when unsure
* Dativ articles: dem (m), der (f), dem (n), den (pl)
* Akkusativ articles: den (m), die (f), das (n), die (pl)
   * Only masculine changes from Nominativ to Akkusativ (der becomes den)
* Sentence pattern with dative verb: subject + verb + dative object + accusative object
   * e.g. "Ich gebe dem Mann das Buch" (I give the man the book)
   * e.g. "Wir kaufen dem Lehrer ein Geschenk" (We buy the teacher a present)

Genitiv

* Always signals possession ("of somebody/something")
   * e.g. "Das Auto des Bruders" (the brother's car)
   * Easy to spot by context; no confusion with Dativ/Akkusativ
* Genitiv articles: des (m), der (f), des (n), der (pl)
* Genitive male and neuter: add an S (or ES) to the end of the noun
   * e.g. des Mannes, des Hauses, des Autos
   * Female and plural: no ending added to the noun
* Defined: des/der/des; Undefined: eines (m), einer (f), eines (n)
